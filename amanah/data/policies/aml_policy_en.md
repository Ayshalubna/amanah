# Sample Bank — AML/CFT Operating Procedures (illustrative)

> Fictional internal procedures written for the Amanah demo. They are modelled
> on the general structure of UAE AML/CFT obligations under Federal Decree-Law
> No. (20) of 2018 but are not legal advice and not an official text.

## 1. Customer due diligence (CDD)
Before opening an account, staff must identify and verify every customer using a valid, unexpired identity document. For UAE residents this is the Emirates ID. The name, date of birth and nationality on the document must match the application. An expired document, an invalid Emirates ID number or a mismatch between the Arabic and English names must be resolved before the account is opened.

## 2. Enhanced due diligence (EDD)
Enhanced due diligence applies to politically exposed persons (PEPs), customers linked to high-risk jurisdictions, and customers whose activity is inconsistent with their declared income or business. EDD requires senior management approval, verification of source of funds and source of wealth, and more frequent account review.

## 3. Sanctions and targeted financial sanctions screening
All customers and counterparties are screened against the UN Security Council Consolidated List and the UAE Local Terrorist List at onboarding and whenever the lists change. A confirmed match requires freezing the funds without delay and without prior notice, and reporting the action to the competent authority. A possible match must be investigated using date of birth, nationality and other identifiers before any decision; the customer must not be informed of the screening result.

## 4. Transaction monitoring red flags
Investigators should consider the following indicators:
- Structuring: repeated cash deposits just below a reporting threshold, such as several deposits under AED 55,000 in a short period.
- Rapid movement of funds: incoming transfers that are sent onwards within hours or days, leaving little balance.
- High-risk jurisdictions: transfers to or from countries identified as high risk for money laundering or terrorist financing.
- Income mismatch: account inflows far above the customer's declared income or expected business turnover.
- Dormant account activity: an inactive account that suddenly receives or moves large round amounts.
A single indicator is not proof of wrongdoing. Legitimate explanations such as a property sale, a salary bonus or a cash-intensive business must be considered and documented.

## 5. Suspicious transaction reports (STR)
When there are reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are proceeds of crime or related to terrorist financing, the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) files a suspicious transaction report with the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit through the goAML platform. The report must describe the customer, the transactions, the red flags observed and why they are suspicious. Staff must not tip off the customer that a report is being considered or has been filed.

## 6. Record keeping and audit trail
All screening results, alerts, investigator decisions and the reasons for them must be recorded and retained for at least five years. Every automated recommendation must show who approved or rejected it and when.

## 7. Use of automated and AI tools
Automated tools may prioritise alerts, draft case summaries and suggest dispositions. They must not freeze funds, close alerts, or file reports on their own. A trained investigator reviews every recommendation and remains accountable for the final decision.
